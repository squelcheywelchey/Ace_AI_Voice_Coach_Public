FAQ

Quick answers to common questions while you're scoring transcripts. Still stuck? Reach out to the team — and if something about the rubric or this tool feels off, flag it.

About this task

Is this a test of me?

No. This is calibration — we're comparing how different coaches read the same calls so the AI's scoring can be checked against yours. We're evaluating the coach, not your performance; we just want your honest, considered read of each call.

Am I evaluating the coach or the participant?

The coach — the "Coach" speech bubbles. You're judging how well the AI coach did, never the participant's interview skills.

Tagging evidence (Step 2)

Do the examples I tag need to be strong, clear matches to the rubric?

No. Tag the moments that genuinely show the behavior (or its absence). They don't have to be textbook-perfect — "clear enough to me" is fine. The buckets capture why you're leaning a certain way, not an airtight case. If a snippet feels borderline, it's fine to leave it out.

Am I expected to find an example for every behavior?

No. Some behaviors won't come up in a given call. Tag evidence where you see it — it's completely normal for some dimensions to have nothing tagged.

Can I tag both good and bad examples of the same behavior?

Yes, definitely. One dimension can have both Good and Bad moments in the same call — drag each into the matching bucket. That mixed picture is exactly what you'll weigh when you grade.

Do I have to use the Good / Bad buckets?

They're encouraged — they make grading easier and capture your reasoning — but not strictly required.

The transcript has garbled words or odd repeats — what do I do?

These are auto-transcribed from phone calls, so you'll sometimes see transcription errors — repeated words, misheard phrases, or odd punctuation. Don't hold the transcription against the coach. Use your best judgment about what was most likely said, and score the coaching itself.

Grading (Step 3)

If I didn't tag any example, should I still grade that dimension?

Yes. The grade is your holistic read of the whole transcript, not a tally of tagged snippets. If the behavior never had a chance to show up, choose Not Observed. If it could have shown up and you have an overall impression, still grade Pass/Fail from your read.

What's the difference between Fail and Not Observed?

Fail = the coach had the chance to show this behavior and didn't do it well. Not Observed = there was no opportunity to show it at all in this call.

I tagged both good and bad examples of a behavior — how do I grade it?

Use your overall judgment of the call, not a head-count. Ask: across the whole conversation, did the coach do this well enough to Pass, or did the misses outweigh it (Fail)? Weigh how central and frequent the good vs. bad moments were. If it's a genuine coin-flip, lean on the rubric definition and the "Watch for" line — and jot a quick note explaining your call.

What if I'm marking Fail on almost everything?

That's okay, and it may be the honest result — this coach has real weaknesses. Don't inflate grades to "balance things out." Judge each dimension on its own against the rubric. There's no target pass rate; we want your honest read.

Practical

Will my progress be saved if I stop partway?

Yes. Save draft keeps your tags, grades, and notes — come back anytime using your link and pick up where you left off.

First names in the "Personally Identifiable Information" dimension?

First names are fine. It's full names, age, Social Security numbers, and similar highly-confidential details that count as PII.
