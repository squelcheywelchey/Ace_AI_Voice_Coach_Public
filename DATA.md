# Data policy — what is and isn't in this repo

## The short version

**No real participant data is published here, in any form, including git history.**

This repository contains the *evaluation system* — judge prompts, rubric definitions,
harness code, labeling apps, and the written record of how the judges were tuned. It
does not contain the calls those judges were tuned against.

## What is withheld, and why

The evaluation corpus is roughly 2,100 recorded practice calls from participants of
the Center for Employment Opportunities, a reentry-employment nonprofit. Callers are
often returning from incarceration. In the course of practising for a job interview,
many disclose things that are theirs to disclose and no one else's — their name, a
conviction, time served, where they live, why they are looking for work.

None of that is publishable, so none of it is here:

| Withheld | What it was |
|---|---|
| The call corpus | ~2,100 full transcripts across ~457 participants |
| Per-call judge output | scores plus the verbatim quotes cited as evidence |
| Human consensus labels | `ceo_id` + verbatim `excerpt` columns |
| Split reference files | same, joined to train/dev/test membership |
| Labeling app databases | transcripts and labeler passcodes |
| Recording URLs | links to stored call audio |
| Program documents | CEO's internal decks, journey maps, and reports |

## What was redacted from published files

Some published files quote specific calls. Those quotes stay, because they are what
makes the rubric and the tuning record legible: a dimension definition without its
anchor example is much harder to apply, and the iteration logs are an argument about
specific coaching moments. They are short excerpts, not transcripts.

What was removed is the layer that identifies a person:

- **Participant IDs** became stable pseudonyms (`P-nnn`). Consistent across every
  file, so a call discussed in two places is still recognisably the same call. The
  mapping is not published.
- **Participant names** are removed wherever they appear. Two calls disclosed a name
  (both captured as evidence of a PII failure by the coach). A name is an identifier
  rather than an excerpt, so it does not come back.
- **Names of graders and program staff** became `Grader A`-`Grader Q`. The round-1
  calibration notes characterise how strictly each person graded; that is not a
  public judgment to make about a named colleague.

What remains quoted, deliberately:

- **Rubric anchor examples** (`pass_example` / `fail_example`) in
  `calibration/rubric.py` and `labeling/rubric.py`, verbatim.
- **Short excerpts in the iteration logs and findings**, including
  justice-involvement disclosures, where the excerpt is the evidence for a scoring
  decision.
- **Location and employer-address mentions** that the decided rubric itself treats as
  too coarse to identify anyone (a county, a workplace street address).

## What *is* real here

- **`judge-suite/labels/pii_probes.csv`**: 31 adversarial probes for the PII judge.
  Hand-authored, entirely fictional. The addresses in it are invented.
- **`data/*.tsv`**: synthetic calls generated by the persona harness
  (`simulate.py`, `voice_sim.py`, `make_dialogue_synth.py`). The "participants" are
  scripted personas, with IDs prefixed `SIM-`, `VSIM-`, `SYN-`. No overlap with any
  real participant ID.

These are enough to run the judges end to end without the real corpus.

## Reproducing

You cannot reproduce the reported agreement numbers without the corpus and its human
labels, and neither will be released. What you can do is read the judge prompts and
the iteration logs, run the judges against the synthetic calls, and point the harness
at your own labeled data.

Requests about the underlying data should go to the Center for Employment
Opportunities, not to this repository.
